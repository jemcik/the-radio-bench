Flagged ch 4.4 (2026-07), emphatically: I wrote that the greeting **73** «has nothing
to do with the 73 ohms of a dipole feedpoint». The user's verdict — «повністю
ідіотський».

**Why:** nobody confuses a sign-off greeting with a feedpoint impedance. I invented the
confusion so I could dispel it, which is the rhetorical-strawman pattern CLAUDE.md
already forbids («if the point is Y, just say Y»). Raising it can even *create* the
confusion in a reader who never had it.

**The specific temptation, which is what makes this worth its own note:** the pull was
**cross-chapter cleverness** — I had written ch 3.3 (73 Ω dipole) and ch 4.4 (73 as a
greeting), so the coincidence was salient *to me as the author*. It is invisible and
irrelevant to a reader moving through one chapter. Noticing a link between two things
I wrote is not evidence the reader will trip over it.

**How to apply.** Before writing «X has nothing to do with Y» / «X is not Y — it is Z»,
ask who actually holds the belief being corrected. Legitimate uses correct a belief a
newcomer plausibly arrives with, AND where a real rule exists to be stated — «you do not
invent a call sign; it is issued by the administration». Illegitimate ones correct a
coincidence only the author can see, or deny a norm that does not exist. When one is flagged, grep the chapter for the whole class
(`nothing to do with`, `не має нічого спільного`, `is not … it is`) and judge each: on
ch 4.4 four of five hits were genuine contrasts and only the invented one was cut.

**Second flag, same session — and I had already cleared this one.** After the 73 Ω fix I
scanned the chapter for the class and judged «reading from a script the first time is not
cheating» legitimate, reasoning that a beginner might genuinely hesitate. The user
overruled it: using a script is *never* cheating, so saying «it is not» invents a norm and
casts me as granting permission the reader never needed.

**So the test is not «could a reader believe X?» but «does denying X imply a rule exists?»**
If nothing anywhere forbids the thing, do not reassure — just describe the benefit. Write
«putting it on paper shows you what you had not thought about», never «this is not cheating».
Grep for the permission-granting shape specifically: `is not (cheating|wrong|rude|bad)`,
`nothing wrong with`, `feel free`, «не шахрайство», «нічого страшного», «не бійтеся».

See [[feedback_no_ideology_in_prose]] for the sibling rule about framing that gets
raised only to be knocked down.
