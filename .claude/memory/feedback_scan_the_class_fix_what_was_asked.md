Two separate failures in one ch4.5 review, in opposite directions. Both made
the user furious, and the resolution is not «fix more» or «fix less».

**Direction 1 — claimed consistency I had not measured.** He flagged one
paragraph where the class letters A/B/C were bold in one sentence and plain in
the next. I fixed that paragraph, then wrote that the convention now held
chapter-wide. It did not: `Стаття 25`, `НКЕК`, `УДЦР` and the class letters
were each styled two ways across the chapter. He found the next instance
immediately. Same shape as «ліцензійний папір», the em-dash before «якщо», and
Ukraine-attached-to-northern-Asia — each fix locally right, blind to its
neighbours.

**Direction 2 — swept without being asked.** Having learned «check the class»,
I then *removed all bold from the whole chapter* — 9 tags per locale — when he
had asked why one sentence differed. «я тебе хіба попросив прибрати всі жирні
шрифти???» Had to restore it all. This is [[feedback-proofread-fast-minimal-fixes]]
being violated by the very attempt to fix thoroughly.

**How to apply — the two are not in tension:**
1. **Always scan** the whole chapter (both locales) for the flagged class
   before saying anything about scope. A grep costs seconds and is the only way
   to know whether the report is «one spot» or «systemic».
2. **Report what the scan found**, including instances you are not touching.
3. **Fix only what was asked**, unless the class is a hard rule violation
   (blaming the reader, ideology, a wrong legal fact) — those get fixed
   everywhere in the same pass, per [[feedback-no-condescension-explain-standards]].
4. **Never claim «consistent everywhere»** without having measured it. Say what
   you checked and what you found; don't generalise from one repair.
5. A convention invented *after* the fix to justify it is rationalisation. If
   there is no rule in the codebase, say so instead of inventing one.
